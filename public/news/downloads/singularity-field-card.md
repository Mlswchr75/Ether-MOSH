# Ether-MOSH Singularity field card

## Direct answer

Singularity mixes the live image with recent finished frames along a wandering log-polar vortex. Pull sets the inward displacement and overall blend, Twist controls rotational shear, and Debris divides the flow into curved shards that dissolve before the center. It is an artistic feedback shader, not a scientific black-hole simulation.

## Starter recipe

- Category: Dimensional
- Pull: 0.32
- Twist: 0.38
- Debris: 0.28
- Source: a portrait, sculpture, architectural still, camera feed, or Forge composition with one clear focal subject
- Optional stack: Film Grain for stills; restrained Bloom for a tested venue loop

## Control map

| Control | Range | What it changes | Practical start |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Pull | 0–1 | Overall blend, inward sampling distance, and upper-range spin and dust escalation; 0 leaves the source untouched | 0.32 |
| Twist | 0–1 | Differential rotation around the automatically wandering center | 0.38 |
| Debris | 0–1 | Log-polar shard density, per-shard offsets, history age, and dissolution radius | 0.28 |

## Production notes

1. Judge the effect while the source is moving; its recent-output feedback is temporal.
2. Keep captions, controls, safety information, and logos outside the effected layer.
3. Freeze a legible still, inspect fine debris at 100%, and proof on the intended paper or textile.
4. Check a 3×3 repeat because the automatically wandering eye may land near a tile edge.
5. Test projection and LED output on the actual surface at full viewing scale.
6. Analyze the delivered loop for flash risk and provide a static or lower-motion alternate.
7. Label observation, physics-based simulation, and artistic shader output separately in education or museum contexts.
